# EXP-005 — First RTX 4090 throughput baseline

On October 12, 2026, a rented Runpod RTX 4090 passed all three boundary-validation cases and all three fixed synthetic benchmark cases. The fastest observed setting reached **2.886 billion candidates/second**. This is a short controlled baseline, not a multi-hour production or puzzle #71 result.

## Results

Each timing case scanned the same half-open synthetic range `[1099511627776,1133871366144)`, containing **34,359,738,368 candidates**. Every case found the positive probe at the final candidate, rejected the probe at the exclusive end, matched the device counter and reached the expected next-key state.

| Profile | Blocks × threads × points | Scan seconds | Billion candidates/s | Initialization seconds |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Small | 64 × 256 × 64 | 27.044650 | 1.270482 | 3.401432 |
| Medium | 128 × 256 × 128 | 13.775809 | 2.494208 | 7.657023 |
| Large | 128 × 256 × 256 | 11.907092 | 2.885653 | 4.120981 |

Large was about 2.27 times faster than small in this run. Each profile was measured once, in fixed order; variance, warm-up/order effects and long-duration thermal stability were not characterized. Initialization is excluded from the reported scan rates. The complete six-case suite took 92.046211 seconds, from `2026-10-12T06:33:26.811757Z` to `2026-10-12T06:34:58.857973Z`.

The three preliminary correctness cases each scanned two complete batches, testing the first candidate, both sides of a batch boundary and the last candidate, plus two negative probes outside the range. All passed. These tiny validation timings are not throughput headline figures.

The host recorded 36 NVIDIA telemetry samples: maximum observed utilization 98%, power 451.18 W and temperature 58°C. Sample timing includes initialization and transitions, so the whole-suite utilization average should not be read as scan-only utilization. NVIDIA reports model `NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090`, driver `595.91.07`.

## Failures and operational overhead

The first local compilation failed on ambiguous uint64 constructors; explicit width casts fixed it before rental. Independent CPU reference checks then passed all 24 target vectors, and four invalid invocation checks passed.

The first allocation request was rejected as out of capacity. A second attempt returned to deployment without allocation; refreshed audit logs showed no new Pod. Refreshing the GPU selection allowed the third attempt to allocate one Pod. No duplicate rental was observed.

The browser session dropped before the benchmark command was submitted. It was restored to the existing Pod, without another allocation. The run itself passed unchanged. Downloading the report through the browser then blocked for approximately **674 seconds**, delaying shutdown and incurring idle rental time. This overhead is included in the lifecycle above and must not be hidden behind the 92-second compute-suite duration. After the original report was preserved locally, the Pod was immediately stopped and deleted. The console text file was not downloaded separately; its completed output is preserved in the screenshot.

## Limits and next decision

This establishes one manual rented-GPU execution of a published bounded worker, correctness probes, measured short-run throughput, sampled activity and verified shutdown. Host output and screenshots can be falsified; they are not cryptographic remote attestation or proof of every individual calculation. Three overlapping runs are not 103 billion distinct candidates covered. **Puzzle #71 candidates searched: zero.**

The large profile is the provisional baseline for the next controlled comparison. Before another paid experiment, improve evidence retrieval and provider shutdown so a blocked browser download cannot keep compute billing. A second GPU model, scaling claim, automated provider lifecycle, live GPU receipt ingestion and real puzzle #71 search remain untested. No recurring or further rental was started.
